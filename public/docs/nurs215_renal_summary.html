Renal Physiology: High-Yield Study Summary

Nephron function · filtration · electrolyte and fluid balance

Study aid for revising renal physiology. It summarizes core mechanisms and is not a clinical protocol. Clinical decisions require current course guidance and qualified supervision.

1. Nephron overview

The nephron filters plasma at the glomerulus, then selectively reabsorbs useful water and solutes and secretes selected substances into the tubular fluid. The final urine volume and composition reflect filtration, reabsorption, and secretion across the nephron.

  • Proximal tubule: bulk reabsorption of filtered sodium, water, bicarbonate, glucose, and amino acids.
  • Loop of Henle: the descending limb is relatively water-permeable; the thick ascending limb reabsorbs salts and is relatively water-impermeable, contributing to the medullary gradient.
  • Distal nephron: fine-tunes sodium, potassium, acid-base balance, and water under hormonal influence.

2. Glomerular filtration

Net filtration pressure depends on glomerular capillary hydrostatic pressure opposed by hydrostatic pressure in Bowman's space and plasma oncotic pressure. The filtration barrier includes fenestrated endothelium, basement membrane, and podocyte slit structures. Changes in renal blood flow and afferent/efferent arteriolar resistance alter filtration dynamics.

4. Acid-base handling

The kidney reclaims filtered bicarbonate, secretes hydrogen ions, and generates new bicarbonate while excreting acid as ammonium and titratable acids. Respiratory changes can compensate rapidly; renal adaptation is slower and adjusts net acid excretion over time.
